> **PFO may be commercially valuable if it allows a complex hybrid medical practice to coordinate and
> complete its clinical, administrative, financial and commercial work with materially less friction,
> latency, hidden work and management uncertainty than its current operating model and viable

**Status: `UNKNOWN`. It is falsifiable. That is the point.**

**The question to ask from now on:**

# 6. METRIC 17 — MANAGEMENT VISIBILITY (formalised)

**Potentially the most important measurement in the entire commercial program**, because it tests
operating *transparency* rather than features.

**Procedure at time T:**
1. Ask the owner/manager independently: *"List everything currently overdue, blocked, waiting or at
   risk that requires staff action."*
2. **Freeze that response** (timestamp, written).
3. **Independently audit** the underlying systems and work sources.
4. Establish the **ground-truth actionable outstanding-work set**.
5. Compare.

**Calculate:**
- **Management Visibility Recall** = `items correctly known / actual actionable outstanding items`
- **Hidden Work Count** = `actual − correctly known`
- **High-Risk Hidden Work** = hidden items involving **clinical safety · patient delay · revenue ·
  compliance · inventory expiration · missed follow-up**

**Repeat identically after PFO.** Target form of evidence:
> *"Before PFO, management could readily identify 14 of 31 actionable exceptions (45%). After PFO, 29
> of 30 (97%)."*

**Those numbers are illustrative format only — nothing has been measured. Do not fabricate them.**

# 7. ROLE-LEVEL BASELINE — aggregates must not hide harm

**PFO may improve one role while making another worse.** Measure separately:
**Front Desk** (registration, scheduling, check-in, eligibility, payments) · **MA/Clinical Assistant**
(intake, rooming, orders/results follow-up, refills) · **Provider** (encounter prep, documentation,
orders, signing, follow-up) · **Biller** (charge review, scrub, rejections, denials, ERA, patient
balance) · **Owner/Manager** (visibility, exceptions, workload, financial risk, inventory,
outstanding work).

**Separate touch time from latency.** Encounter→signed-note may be 4 hours elapsed and 7 minutes of
provider work. **Those measure different problems, and the Control Tower may improve queue visibility
without changing touch time at all.** Record both.

# 9. POSITION ON THE STANDING QUESTIONS

**Certification** — keep the structural conclusion (the ACB invoice is the smallest bucket;
engineering, permanent maintenance and opportunity cost dominate). **Next work is the Certification
Dependency Map, NOT starting certification.** Per customer type: must PFO itself be certified, could
another certified module satisfy it, or is CEHRT dependency immaterial?
**Certification buys access, not product-market fit.**

**Payments** — `TECHNICAL CAPABILITY: SUPPORTED` · `REVENUE ECONOMICS: UNKNOWN`. Questions owed to
Square/Stripe: economic participation · referral vs platform vs Connect economics · merchant
architecture · **who owns underwriting, KYC, chargebacks, reserves, PCI responsibility** · contract
minimums · revenue share · volume tiers · surcharge/markup restrictions · HIPAA/BAA.
**Until answered: $0 assumed margin.**

**Home health** — **`PARKED ADJACENCY`. Do not consume engineering capacity.** Medicare-certified
requires OASIS, PDGM, EVV, field scheduling/routing, offline mobile, CoP. Private-duty still requires
workforce scheduling PFO does not have. **Revisit only after repeatability in the core market.**

**Delivery capacity** — *a clinic buying an EHR is buying an organization, not software.* Track for
PFO itself: implementation/configuration/migration/training hours · support tickets/week and response
time · integration work · engineering escalations · time to go-live · ongoing load.
**A product producing 30% clinic improvement that consumes six months of founder time per
implementation is not scalable.** Measure at ReCenter and especially at customer #2.

**Company vs ReCenter operating asset** — **do not force the choice today.** Define what evidence
would trigger commercialisation: ReCenter measurable improvement · customer #2 willingness ·
successful #2 implementation · manageable support burden · established migration model · pricing
willingness · processor economics · certification dependency understood.
**Until then ReCenter is both production customer and evidence generator.**

## R2.5 — External customer #2 remains the hard item

**Medical and OASIS are NOT customer #1 and customer #2.** For commercial evidence, reserve
**"first independent external customer"** for an organisation **outside the ReCenter relationship
that independently chooses PFO.**

Two practices under one owner, one building and one decision-maker **do not test** willingness to buy,
implementation at an unfamiliar site, support at a distance, or configuration by someone who did not
build the system.

## R2.6 — Baseline measurement structure (updated — supersedes the flat role-only model)

**Measure on TWO dimensions, never collapsed:**

```
PRACTICE:  Medical · OASIS · (Home Health when live)
ROLE:      Front Desk · MA · Provider · Practitioner · Biller · Manager
```

**Medical and OASIS get separate baseline and post-PFO results wherever workflows differ.**
**Do not report clinic-wide averages that obscure differences between the two practice models** — the
whole platform claim lives in that difference.

**Note the role list gained `Practitioner`** — OASIS's aesthetics clinician is a distinct role from
Medical's provider, and merging them would hide exactly the comparison that matters.

**Why this matters commercially:** the achievable claim is no longer
~~"PFO improved ReCenter"~~ but potentially:
> **"PFO produced measurable improvement across two materially different practice models using the
> same underlying platform."**

**That is a far stronger evidence of platform generality**, and it costs nothing extra beyond
recording which practice each observation came from. **The baseline workbook must carry a PRACTICE
field on every metric card.**
