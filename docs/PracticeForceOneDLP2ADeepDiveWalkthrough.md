## The Documentation Engine, in plain terms

This is the part to slow down on. Nothing above asked the provider to remember anything —
and that is the product.

1. **The chart becomes evidence.** Every vital, diagnosis, medication, allergy, consent and
   document on this visit was distilled into a set of concrete facts (they are all listed at
   the end of this document — nothing hidden).
2. **The practice's own rules read the evidence.** The practice’s rule pack runs over those facts.
   These are THIS practice's rules — a different practice with different medicine gets
   different rules from the same engine, with no custom code.
3. **What fired becomes actions, not paperwork.** Recommendations reach the provider — each one
   titled by the ACTION to take, with why it matters, what happens if ignored, and one
   button that goes exactly where the work is done.
4. **Completing work is watched, not reported.** File the document and the recommendation
   closes itself; the progress bar moves; the remaining count drops. Nobody ticks a box.
5. **The signature is guarded.**  The
   encounter cannot enter the legal record while required safety work is open — and the
   refusal names each item, so it is a to-do list, not a wall.

**One engine, both practices.** The medical clinic's bleeding-risk and kidney-safety rules
and the aesthetics clinic's lot-tracking and consent rules are DATA, not code. That is the
platform claim: new specialty, new rules, same engine, same afternoon.

**The mental model to present (founder's framing).** This is a CLINICAL WORK engine, not a
document engine. The provider reviews medications, screens for depression, records consent,
verifies allergies — clinical work. Documents are the artifacts that work produces, the way
claims are artifacts of coding and invoices are artifacts of billing. The provider never
"creates a document": they complete clinical activities, and the platform transforms that
work into the documentation, coding, billing, quality and operational records the practice
needs — then tells them what remains, and guards the signature until nothing does.
