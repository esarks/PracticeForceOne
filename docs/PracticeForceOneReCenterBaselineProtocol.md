# PART 2 — THE RECENTER BASELINE PROTOCOL *(Priority G — urgent)*

**This is the only priority requiring no external research, and it is the one with a deadline.**

> **Every day PFO rolls out further, the BEFORE state is destroyed permanently.** A baseline not
> captured now can never be reconstructed, and without it the only durable commercial asset available
> to PFO — *"here is what changed"* — cannot be produced at any price.

**This outranks every remaining competitive teardown**, because the teardowns can be done any time and
this cannot.

## Method notes (read before measuring)

- **Observe, do not survey.** Ask a person how long check-in takes and you get an estimate. Watch ten
  check-ins with a stopwatch and you get a baseline.
- **Record narratives alongside numbers.** *"The biller keeps a sticky note of claims to re-check"* is
  evidence, and it is the kind that survives into a sales conversation.
- **Count surfaces honestly** — a spreadsheet, a paper log and a second browser tab each count as a
  system.
- **Capture N and date on every row.** A baseline without a sample size is an anecdote.
- **Do not tune anything while measuring.** Improving the process during baseline destroys the
  comparison.

## The protocol

| # | Metric | Baseline source | Period | Minimum meaningful change |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Systems touched per patient journey** | direct observation, count distinct apps/sheets/paper | 10 journeys | **−2 systems** |
| 2 | **Screens/worklists checked per role per day** | shadow each role | 3 days × 5 roles | **−30%** |
| 3 | **Manual handoffs per journey** | observation | 10 journeys | **−2 handoffs** |
| 4 | **Paper / sticky-note / spreadsheet artefacts in use** | physical inventory | one-time census | **−50%** |
| 5 | **Check-in time** (arrival → ready for provider) | stopwatch | 20 check-ins | **−25%** |
| 6 | **Eligibility handling effort** | time + touches per exception | 10 exceptions | **−40%** |
| 7 | **Encounter preparation time** | observation | 10 encounters | **−25%** |
| 8 | **Provider documentation time** | observation | 10 encounters | **−20%** |
| 9 | **Encounter → signed note** | timestamps *(PFO can measure; capture the current state now)* | 30 days | **−50%** |
| 10 | **Signed note → charge/clean claim** | timestamps + biller log | 30 days | **−50%** |
| 11 | **Claim exception handling effort** | minutes per exception + touches | 20 exceptions | **−30%** |
| 12 | **Patient payment reconciliation effort** | minutes/day | 2 weeks | **−50%** |
| 13 | **Inventory reconciliation effort + discrepancy rate** | count-day observation | 1 cycle | **−50% effort** |
| 14 | **Expired product loss** | $ written off | 1 quarter *(start now)* | **−50%** |
| 15 | **Supplement / resupply workflow effort** | observation | 10 orders | **−40%** |
| 16 | **Recall workflow effort + conversion rate** | log review | 1 cycle | **+20% conversion** |
| 17 | **Outstanding work management cannot readily see** | ask the owner to list it, then audit against reality | one-time | **the headline number** |

**Row 17 is the most commercially valuable measurement in this document.** If the owner cannot name
what is overdue and an audit finds material hidden work, **that gap — measured, in their own clinic —
is the control tower's justification.** It is also the only metric that directly tests the surviving
thesis, because it measures *operating transparency* rather than features.

## What this produces

> **"At ReCenter, PFO reduced X from A to B."**

**Competitors can match a feature list within a release cycle. They cannot match your clinic's
measured before-and-after.** After three rounds of falsification, this is the only asset identified
that no competitor can copy.
